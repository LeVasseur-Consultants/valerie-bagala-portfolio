Why TerraFirma

Infrastructure Is the Bottleneck — Efficiency Is the Answer

The pull isn't "fast-paced startup." It's more specific than that: I want to be inside the company building the layer everything else depends on, not optimizing a supply chain that already exists. That's the same instinct that has shaped my career — embedding inside engineering rather than sitting downstream of it, going to the field to understand an operation before I'll build a contract around it, and treating purchasing as a function that should make engineers faster, not just keep them supplied.
A belief that shapes how I'd approach this role specifically: tactical task execution is the layer that gets absorbed by better tools and, eventually, AI. What doesn't get replaced is someone who understands the process deeply enough to make the right call, build the right relationship, and translate that into bottom-line value. That's the function I'd want to build here — a support system for engineers, not a request queue.

Kitting Assumptions
Working assumption, not confirmed by TerraFirma: the fastest path to scale here is retrofitting existing heavy equipment — excavators, loaders, and similar — with autonomy-enabling hardware, rather than manufacturing new machines from scratch. That's the standard approach in this space when speed-to-field matters more than a clean-sheet build, and it fits a company deploying into complex international environments quickly.

A Real Example

What Indecision Actually Costs

An end-of-life component needed a $100K bridge buy to carry production through a redesign. Approval stalled — not for technical or budget reasons, but because no one wanted to own the decision. The need never went away, so the outcome was forced anyway: a broker-market buy at $500K.

$100K
The fix that was available, and needed, from the start
$500K
What it actually cost once forced by delay
$400K
Paid entirely for indecision, not for the component itself

The need was never in question. The only thing that changed while we waited was the price. That's the gap I try to close — not by moving faster than is responsible, but by making sure a correct call doesn't sit unmade.
