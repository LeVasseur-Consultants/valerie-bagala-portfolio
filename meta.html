Why Meta

Infrastructure Is the Foundation — the Program Is What Keeps It Standing

The pull isn't "big tech" as a category — it's more specific than that: data centers are infrastructure in the most literal sense, and a supplier strategy program for that footprint either becomes the thing that lets Facility Operations move fast and stay accountable, or the thing that slows it down. That's the same instinct that's shaped my career — building the governance layer other teams depend on rather than sitting downstream of it, and treating a supplier program as something that should make site teams faster, not just keep them supplied.
A belief that shapes how I'd approach this role specifically: a supplier strategy program only works when it's built as infrastructure, not policy — a standard someone can run without me in the room, backed by a process people actually use because it's faster than the workaround. That's the muscle I've built repeatedly: owning the requisition-to-purchase-order process end to end, and converting a 100-page manual governance process into a single automated global workflow with no mandate to do it, just because the gap was there.
How I work with people, not just process: I'm extremely independent, but I'm also a true team player — I genuinely want to help the people around me, because I know our success is intrinsically linked to the company's bottom line, and I never lose sight of that. I'm a passionate optimist, even in the face of adversity, and I bring that energy into how I work. I'd describe myself as a blend of old-school, foundational experience and forward-thinking practice — always looking for ways to leverage technology to cut down decision and change time, and to make things better, easier, and more efficient.

How I'd Start

The First 90 Days

Before I build anything, I want to understand what's actually true here first — the same instinct behind every framework above: learn the real process before changing it.

Days 1–30 · Learn the Real Program

Map what's actually running today across supplier lifecycle, category management, and contract governance for fleetwide services — where standards exist, where they're inconsistent, and where PR-to-PO friction is costing time. Meet with Sourcing, Operations, Legal, and Enterprise Engineering to find what's urgent and where duplicative effort is hiding.

Days 31–60 · Close the Fastest Gaps

Tighten the business review and audit cadence for supplier performance and compliance, and fix the one or two process breakpoints causing the most escalations at the site level. Look for a fast, visible win rather than months of framework-building before anything ships.

Days 61–90 · Make It Repeatable

Formalize what worked into a documented standard multiple teams can run without me in the room, with a clear implementation, compliance, and communication plan — the same instinct behind the Zeiss Power App and the Shell RtP governance model — so the program scales past the first fire it puts out.

Closing Note

I'm a dog with a bone about the things I believe are worth proving.

When I can show a case is a definitive benefit, I chip away until it's actually adopted — not just proposed. That's the habit behind every framework and workflow above, and it's the habit I'd bring to running supplier strategy at Meta: understand the process, prove the case, see it through.
