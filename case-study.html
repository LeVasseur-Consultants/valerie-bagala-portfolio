Objective

From Reactive Buying to a Managed Category

Category Segmentation

Four Board Families, Four Different Manufacturing Profiles

Rather than sourcing PCBA as one category, the spend was segmented by board complexity and manufacturing process — each with a different sourcing approach.

Standard / Commodity PCBA

High-volume, low-layer-count boards. Lean SMT lines, short lead times, and strong cost efficiency at scale.

Complex / Multi-Layer PCBA

Higher layer counts, tighter tolerances, and controlled-impedance routing. Greater engineering complexity and DFM involvement.

Optical & Sensor-Integrated PCBA

Boards integrated with optical, laser, or sensor components. Highest engineering complexity, lower volume, tightly controlled process.

Cable & Harness Assembly

Wiring harnesses and cable assemblies supporting board-level systems. Hand-assembly, mixed connector types, and system integration testing.

Recommendation

A Tiered Strategy, Not the Lowest Bid

The pure lowest-price scenario carried the highest supplier-concentration risk. The recommendation instead balanced savings against capability fit and continuity of supply.

Tier 1 & 2 — Strategic / Preferred (70–100% of volume)

Complex & Multi-Layer PCBA Partner
Strategy: Grow
Strong existing partnership with proven quality and capacity.
Global EMS Partner (Multi-Site)
Strategy: Implement → Grow
Strong domestic capability plus global network, supporting international expansion.
Optical / Sensor Integration Specialist
Strategy: Implement → Grow
Strong technical and engineering capability, with backup capacity in standard boards.

Tier 3 — Approved / Planned Exit

Three Legacy EMS Incumbents
Strategy: Maintain → Decline / Exit
Retained short-term for continuity across standard boards, cable assemblies, and legacy programs; phased exit as Tier 1/2 volume ramps.
Transition Safeguard
3-month safety stock built into exit planning
Existing contracts included 30-day termination for convenience, giving a clean off-ramp.
Transition Plan

Phased Rollout Over 12 Months

Short Term (0–4 mo)

  • Finalize Tier 1/2/3 supplier designations
  • Select top-savings board families to transition first, by sub-category
  • Maintain Tier 2/3 base throughout transition

Mid Term (4–8 mo)

  • Evaluate new Tier 1 supplier process and execution
  • Confirm product quality against standard
  • Set remaining 2018 volume allocation by sub-category

Long Term (8–12 mo)

  • Exit Tier 3 suppliers per transition safeguard
  • Full implementation of Tier 1/2 by sub-category
  • Evaluate performance ahead of next-year volume allocation
