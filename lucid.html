How I'd run the category

Accessories need more than one sourcing model

A floor mat, a charging adapter and a branded lifestyle item carry different risks and different brand stakes. I'd segment the portfolio first and source each tier on its own terms.

Tier 1 · Premium, high complexity

Technical & vehicle-matched

Adapters, trim-matched parts, OEM-grade replacements
One premium partner, Lucid design control

Tooling ownership or right-of-use secured in the contract, a qualified backup identified before launch, and quality gates owned jointly with Engineering.

Tier 2 · Standard

Fit-and-finish essentials

Floor mats, cargo solutions, covers
Dual source, regional where it pays

Two qualified suppliers to protect availability and keep price honest, with the regional vs. global split set by lead time, tariffs and service demand.

Tier 3 · Lifestyle

Brand and lifestyle

Merchandise, travel and lifestyle items
Competitive, multi-supplier

Regular competitive bids against a written brand-standards spec, so suppliers compete on price and service, not on quality.

Step 1 · Before award

No surprises

RFQs that spell out who covers tooling, NRE, MOQs and warranty. Cost builds from 2–3 qualified suppliers, checked against my own should-cost.

Step 2 · Award

Price, then structure

Piece price against should-cost, then tooling ownership, NRE capped and tied to milestones, and MOQ runoff terms that protect end-of-life inventory.

Step 3 · Contract & after

Governed for life

MSA and SOW with clear warranty split, committed lead times and annual cost-down targets, then scorecards and QBRs from the first shipment.

On cost vs. brand: I won't source a floor mat that feels cheap to save 20%. Cost reduction should come from process, volume, logistics and supplier capability, never from how the product looks or feels in an owner's hands.
