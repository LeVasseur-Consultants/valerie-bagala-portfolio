Mission-Driven, Regulated, and Scaling Fast — the Environment I've Spent a Career Inside Of

The pull isn't "return to pharma" as a category — it's more specific than that: Gilead is investing aggressively right now, committing over $11B into new oncology and inflammation platforms through the Arcellx, Tubulis, and Ouro Medicines acquisitions, while its core HIV and Commercial business just posted its strongest second-quarter growth in three years. That's exactly the kind of environment where a Category Management function either becomes the thing that lets the business move fast, or the thing that slows it down. I've spent my career building the former.
A belief that shapes how I'd approach this role specifically: spend governance only works when it's built as infrastructure, not policy. A procurement-involvement threshold only earns real compliance when the person enforcing it can show their work in dollars and risk, not just cite a rule. That's the muscle I've built repeatedly — most recently owning requisition-to-payment governance for Carl Zeiss Meditec's full indirect spend base, and before that architecting an 8-stage supplier qualification framework with no existing playbook to follow.
On the 12-month engagement format: every framework on this page — the 8-stage supplier qualification model, the broker-market program, the Source-to-Pay governance buildout — was built inside a defined window, not as an open-ended mandate. A scoped engagement isn't a mismatch with how I work; it's closer to the format I've delivered in most often.
How I work with people, not just process: I'm extremely independent, but I'm also a true team player — I genuinely want to help the people around me, because I know our success is intrinsically linked to the company's bottom line, and I never lose sight of that. I'm a passionate optimist, even in the face of adversity, and I bring that energy into how I work. I'd describe myself as a blend of old-school, foundational experience and forward-thinking practice — always looking for ways to leverage technology to cut down decision and change time, and to make things better, easier, and more efficient.

A Real Example

What Indecision Actually Costs

An end-of-life component needed a $100K bridge buy to carry production through a redesign. Approval stalled — not for technical or budget reasons, but because no one wanted to own the decision. The need never went away, so the outcome was forced anyway: a broker-market buy at $500K.

The need was never in question. The only thing that changed while we waited was the price. That's the gap I try to close — not by moving faster than is responsible, but by making sure a correct call doesn't sit unmade.

How I'd Approach It

A 12-Month Engagement, Built to Leave Something Behind

Every framework on this page was built inside a defined window, not an open-ended mandate — this is that same approach applied to a scoped, 12-month engagement.

Months 1–2 · Learn the Real Spend Base

Map what's actually being spent today across the assigned category, who owns which supplier relationships, and where governance is inconsistent. Meet with Legal, Finance, Security, and business stakeholders to find what's urgent and where the procurement-involvement threshold is being bypassed.

Months 3–8 · Execute the Core Mandate

Run the RFx and consolidation opportunities that deliver the fastest, most defensible value — the same should-cost and supplier-qualification discipline behind the Zeiss and Shell work above — while standing up the tiering, intake, and performance tracking the category needs.

Months 9–12 · Hand Off Something Repeatable

Formalize what worked into a documented playbook — the same instinct behind the 8-stage supplier qualification framework and the Zeiss S2P governance model — so the category strategy outlives the engagement, whether or not it converts to a permanent seat.

Closing Note

I'm a dog with a bone about the things I believe are worth proving.

When I can show a case is a definitive benefit, I chip away until it's actually adopted — not just proposed. That's the habit behind every framework and workflow above, and it's the habit I'd bring to this engagement at Gilead: understand the process, prove the case, see it through.
